Android 12 is the release where the wallpaper picked the colors of the whole
system. For users it brought Material You, a privacy dashboard and switches that
cut off every app's camera and microphone. For developers targeting API 31 it
made `android:exported` and `PendingIntent` mutability mandatory, and it stopped
foreground services from starting in the background.

![The Android 12 wordmark: the word android followed by the number 12, in black](./assets/android-12-logo.svg 'Android 12 logo, shown on a light plate. Wikimedia Commons · Public domain (PD-textlogo) · Trademark of Google LLC')

## At a glance

|                                 |                                                                               |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Version                         | Android 12                                                                    |
| API level                       | 31                                                                            |
| Name                            | Snow Cone, internal codename. Public name: Android 12                         |
| `Build.VERSION_CODES`           | `S`                                                                           |
| Release                         | Pixel rollout began 19 October 2021                                           |
| Launch devices                  | Pixel 6 and Pixel 6 Pro, announced 19 October 2021, in stores from 28 October |
| Google Play target-API deadline | New apps from 1 August 2022, updates from 1 November 2022                     |

Google pushed the Android 12 source to AOSP on 4 October 2021. Phones waited
two more weeks: the rollout started on 19 October, for the Pixel 3 and newer,
the same day Google announced the Pixel 6 and Pixel 6 Pro. Google named Samsung,
OnePlus, Oppo, realme, Tecno, Vivo and Xiaomi for later in the year. The first
build, `SP1A.210812.015`, covered the Pixel 3 to the Pixel 5a.

The codename was Snow Cone. In AOSP the release branches are `sc-dev` and
`sc-release`, the prefix the commits at the `android-12.0.0_r1` tag merge into.
On 4 October 2021 Dave Burke, Android's VP of engineering, posted "A cool
background to celebrate the launch of Android Snow Cone!", which TechRadar and
Android Central reported. The SDK constant stays `S`, and Google never used the
name in its own release posts.

## What it brought

- **Material You.** A new design language with larger controls, a redesigned
  notification shade and Quick Settings, and widgets with rounded corners.
- **Dynamic color.** The system builds a palette from the wallpaper and applies
  it to the lock screen, notifications, settings, widgets and apps that opt in.
  Apps read it through 65 new color resources, five palettes of 13 tones each
  (`system_accent1_0` to `system_neutral2_1000`). Google shipped it on Pixel
  first.
- **Splash screens.** Every app gets a system splash screen on cold and warm
  starts, built from its icon and theme. The `SplashScreen` API customizes it
  with attributes such as `windowSplashScreenAnimatedIcon`.
- **Stretch overscroll.** Lists stretch and bounce at the edge instead of
  showing a glow.
- **Rich content insertion.** One callback, `OnReceiveContentListener`, receives
  images and other content from the clipboard, the keyboard or drag and drop.
- **Graphics and media.** `RenderEffect` blurs and filters a view in one call,
  AVIF images decode natively, and the system can transcode HEVC and HDR video
  to AVC for apps that cannot read it.
- **Notifications.** `Notification.CallStyle` for calls, animated images in
  messaging and big picture styles, and notification actions that require an
  unlocked device (`setAuthenticationRequired()`).
- **Smaller additions.** Rounded corner APIs (`RoundedCorner`), richer haptics
  (`VibratorManager`), a Game Mode API, AppSearch, picture-in-picture that enters
  on its own (`setAutoEnterEnabled()`), and the chipset name in
  `Build.SOC_MANUFACTURER` and `Build.SOC_MODEL`.

Google's developer post said Android 12 cut the CPU time used by core system
services by 22%.

## Security and privacy

- **Privacy dashboard.** A Settings screen shows a timeline of which apps used
  location, camera and microphone in the past 24 hours. An app can explain each
  access with an activity guarded by `START_VIEW_PERMISSION_USAGE` that handles
  `VIEW_PERMISSION_USAGE_FOR_PERIOD`.
- **Camera and microphone indicators.** A status bar icon appears whenever an
  app uses either sensor. AOSP makes the indicators mandatory for all device
  makers.
- **Camera and microphone toggles.** "Camera access" and "Mic access" in Quick
  Settings turn the sensor off for every app. A blocked app gets a blank camera
  feed or silent audio, without an error. Emergency calls turn the microphone
  back on.
- **Clipboard toast.** The first time an app reads another app's clip with
  `getPrimaryClip()`, the system shows "APP pasted from your clipboard".
  `getPrimaryClipDescription()` does not trigger it.
- **Private Compute Core.** An isolated, open source part of the OS where Live
  Caption, Now Playing and Smart Reply process data on the device. Features
  inside it have no direct network access; model updates come through Private
  Compute Services.
- **Overlays.** Touches that pass through an obscuring window are blocked, apps
  can hide other apps' overlays with `HIDE_OVERLAY_WINDOWS`, and preinstalled
  apps no longer get `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` automatically.
- **Platform.** ART becomes a Mainline module (`com.android.art`), so Google can
  update the runtime through Google Play system updates. Rust is supported for
  platform code. New phones ship the Generic Kernel Image, and Ars Technica
  called the Pixel 6 the first GKI phone. Users can also turn off 2G, and work
  profiles lose access to the IMEI and serial number.
- **Crypto.** The deprecated BouncyCastle algorithms, including every AES
  variant, are gone in favor of Conscrypt.

## Permissions

- **Approximate location.** When an app asks for `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`, the
  dialog offers "Precise" or "Approximate", next to the one-time options from
  [Android 11](/notes/android-11/). The app must request
  `ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION` in the same call, or on some Android 12 releases the
  dialog does not appear at all.

![A location permission dialog with two choices, Precise and Approximate, above the buttons While using the app, Only this time, and Deny](./assets/approximate-location-dialog.png 'The Android 12 location dialog, with "Precise" and "Approximate". Android Developers · CC BY 2.5')

- **Nearby devices.** `BLUETOOTH_SCAN`, `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` and
  `BLUETOOTH_ADVERTISE` are new runtime permissions, shown to the user as
  "Nearby devices", along with `UWB_RANGING`. They replace `BLUETOOTH` and
  `BLUETOOTH_ADMIN` for apps targeting 31. An app that never derives location
  from scans adds `usesPermissionFlags="neverForLocation"` and drops
  `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`.
- **Exact alarms.** For apps targeting 31, `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` is an app-op
  permission granted in
  the "Alarms & reminders" special access screen. Call `canScheduleExactAlarms()`
  before setting one; if the user revokes it, the app stops and its exact alarms
  are cancelled.
- **Fast sensors.** For apps targeting 31, the accelerometer, gyroscope and
  magnetometer are capped at 200 Hz through `registerListener()`. Higher rates
  need `HIGH_SAMPLING_RATE_SENSORS`, and with the microphone toggle off the cap
  applies anyway.
- **Hibernation.** For apps targeting 31, the permission auto-reset of Android 11
  grows: an app the user has not opened for a few months also loses its jobs, alarms, push messages and
  cache. The switch is "Remove permissions and free up space" in App info.

![The Location usage screen of the privacy dashboard: a timeline of apps that used location today and yesterday, with a Manage permission button](./assets/privacy-dashboard.png 'The privacy dashboard in Android 12, showing location use over 24 hours. Android Developers · CC BY 2.5')

## What changed for your app

### All apps on Android 12

| Change                                                            | What to do                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| The system shows its own splash screen                            | Move a custom splash activity to the `SplashScreen` API                        |
| Users can pick approximate location                               | Request `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` and `ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION` together           |
| Camera and microphone toggles can block your app silently         | Check `SensorPrivacyManager.supportsSensorToggle()` and expect empty data      |
| Generic web links open your app only for approved domains         | Verify the domain with Android App Links (`https`, `BROWSABLE`)                |
| Back on a launcher root activity moves the task to the background | Call `super.onBackPressed()` instead of `finish()`                             |
| Untrusted touches through overlays are blocked                    | Keep overlays at or below 0.8 combined opacity, or out of the way              |
| `ACTION_CLOSE_SYSTEM_DIALOGS` is deprecated and no longer works   | Drop it; accessibility services use `GLOBAL_ACTION_DISMISS_NOTIFICATION_SHADE` |
| Apps can land in the restricted standby bucket                    | Check `getAppStandbyBucket()` for `STANDBY_BUCKET_RESTRICTED`                  |
| BouncyCastle AES and 512-bit keys are gone                        | Use Conscrypt key sizes and a 12-byte IV for GCM                               |
| `Display.getRealSize()` and `getRealMetrics()` are deprecated     | Use `WindowMetrics`                                                            |

### Apps targeting API 31

| Change                                                                       | What to do                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Components with an intent filter must declare `android:exported`             | Set `true` for the launcher activity and anything others call, `false` otherwise               |
| Every `PendingIntent` must state its mutability                              | Pass `FLAG_IMMUTABLE`, or `FLAG_MUTABLE` only when the intent must be filled in                |
| Foreground services cannot start from the background                         | Use WorkManager expedited work, or an exact alarm for a user-requested action                  |
| Notification trampolines are blocked                                         | Point `setContentIntent()` straight at the activity                                            |
| Exact alarms need `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`                                     | Declare it, check `canScheduleExactAlarms()`, and use exact alarms only for user-facing timing |
| Bluetooth needs the new runtime permissions                                  | Declare and request `BLUETOOTH_SCAN`, `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` or `BLUETOOTH_ADVERTISE`             |
| Custom notification views sit in a system template, 48dp tall when collapsed | Test them in the shade and provide an expanded view                                            |
| Backup rules move to `android:dataExtractionRules`                           | Write separate `<cloud-backup>` and `<device-transfer>` rules                                  |
| `adb backup` leaves app data out                                             | Test backups with a debuggable build                                                           |
| WebView treats cookies without `SameSite` as `Lax`                           | Set `SameSite=None; Secure` on cross-site cookies                                              |
| Vendor native libraries are hidden                                           | Declare each one with `<uses-native-library>`                                                  |

Without `android:exported`, the app does not install on Android 12: the build
fails with a manifest merger error. A missing mutability flag fails at runtime,
and lint flags it as `UnspecifiedImmutableFlag`.

The foreground service rule throws `ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException`.
The exceptions include a high-priority FCM message, a tap on the app's
notification or widget, an exact alarm, `BOOT_COMPLETED`, and apps the user
exempted from battery optimization.

```mermaid title="Starting a foreground service in an app targeting API 31"
flowchart TD
  start([Work must start now]) --> visible{App visible to the user?}
  visible -- yes --> fgs[startForegroundService]
  visible -- no --> exempt{Exemption applies?}
  exempt -- yes --> fgs
  exempt -- no --> work[WorkManager expedited work]
```

Calling `startForegroundService()` anyway on the "no" branch is what throws the
exception.

## What lasted

- **Material You and dynamic color** remain Android's design base, and the 65
  system color resources are still the way apps read the palette.
- **The Nearby devices group** grew: Android 13 added `NEARBY_WIFI_DEVICES`, so
  Wi-Fi scanning no longer needs location either.
- **Exact alarms** tightened. Android 13 added `USE_EXACT_ALARM` for alarm and
  calendar apps and stopped pre-granting `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` to new installs
  of apps targeting 33.
- **Hibernation** stayed. From Android 13 the switch reads "Pause app activity
  if unused".
- **Foreground services** kept narrowing: apps targeting Android 14 must declare
  a type for every one.
- **The splash screen** reached older devices through
  `androidx.core:core-splashscreen`, which brings it to API 23.
- **ART** as a module paid off. In 2023 Google said its ART updates reached all
  Android 12 and newer devices, with app startup up to 30% faster on some.

The point release that followed, [Android 12L](/notes/android-12l/) (API 32), reworked the system for
large screens and has its own note in this series.

## Worth knowing

- The Easter egg is an analog clock. Drag its hands to 12:00 and the next stage
  starts; the code logs "12:00 let's gooooo".
- The background then fills with bubbles drawn from the system accent palette,
  and the egg unlocks **Paint Chips**, a home screen widget that shows the
  dynamic color palette "in a manner similar to a set of paint samples from a
  hardware store", as the AOSP string comment puts it.
- In AOSP the wallpaper-to-theme code sits behind a flag named Monet
  (`isMonetEnabled()` in SystemUI's `ThemeOverlayController`). Ars Technica's
  review reported Monet as the color system's codename.
- The `S` constant in AOSP's `Build.java` carries a one-word comment: `S.`.
