Android 10 is the release where Android stopped naming versions after
desserts. For developers it is the privacy release: apps targeting API 29 see
only their own files on shared storage, background location became a
permission of its own, and apps lost IMEI and serial number. Users got gesture
navigation and a system-wide dark theme.

![The Android 10 home screen: a blue gradient wallpaper, the date and weather at the top, five app icons, the Google search bar and the three-button navigation bar](./assets/android-10-home-screen.png 'Android 10 with the Pixel Launcher and the three-button navigation bar. Wikimedia Commons · Free screenshot')

## At a glance

|                                 |                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Version                         | Android 10                                                                                          |
| API level                       | 29                                                                                                  |
| Name                            | Quince Tart, internal only. Public name: Android 10                                                 |
| `Build.VERSION_CODES`           | `Q`                                                                                                 |
| Final SDK                       | 5 June 2019 (Beta 4, final API 29)                                                                  |
| Release                         | Pixel rollout and AOSP source, 3 September 2019                                                     |
| Launch devices                  | OnePlus 7T, announced 26 September 2019; Google's Pixel 4 and Pixel 4 XL, announced 15 October 2019 |
| Google Play target-API deadline | New apps from 3 August 2020, updates to existing apps from 2 November 2020                          |
| Toolchain                       | `compileSdkVersion 29`, Android Studio 3.3 or later (see below)                                     |

Google's Android 10 SDK setup page says apps compile and test with Android
Studio 3.3 and higher, though some users "may encounter Gradle sync failures".
The release post told developers to use Android Studio 3.5 or higher. No
Google page names a minimum Android Gradle Plugin for API 29; its compatibility
table starts at API 33.

Google published the final API 29 SDK with Beta 4 on 5 June 2019 and opened
Play to apps targeting API 29 the same day. On 3 September it released the
source to AOSP and started the rollout to the Pixel, Pixel 2, Pixel 3 and
Pixel 3a. Android Police reported the rollout the same day. The Essential
Phone and Xiaomi's Redmi K20 Pro also got the update on day one. OnePlus
presented the 7T as the first new phone preloaded with Android 10 with Google's
apps; the Pixel 4 was the first Google phone to ship with it. The February 2023
security bulletin is the last one that lists Android 10 among the patched AOSP
versions; from March 2023 the bulletins start at Android 11. The six Android Q
betas, from 13 March to 7 August 2019, are the parts below this overview.

## What it brought

- **Gesture navigation.** A fully gestural mode replaces the navigation bar:
  swipe up for Home, swipe in from either side for Back. Apps should draw
  edge to edge and handle insets. The three-button bar stays available.
- **Dark theme.** A system-wide dark theme, turned on in Settings or by Battery
  Saver. Apps can ship their own dark theme, or opt in to Force Dark with
  `android:forceDarkAllowed="true"` and let the system darken a light theme.
- **Foldables.** All visible activities in multi-window are resumed, and only
  the "topmost resumed" one has focus (`onTopResumedActivityChanged()`).
  `android:minAspectRatio` is new, and `resizeableActivity` changed meaning.
- **Settings panels.** `Settings.Panel` actions show connectivity, Wi-Fi, NFC
  or volume settings as a floating panel over the app.
- **Sharing Shortcuts.** Apps publish share targets in advance through
  `ShortcutInfo`, so the share sheet loads at once. They replace the Direct
  Share APIs, which still work at a lower priority.
- **Smart Reply in notifications.** The system suggests replies and actions
  with on-device ML. Apps opt out with `setAllowGeneratedReplies()` and
  `setAllowSystemGeneratedContextualActions()`.
- **Live Caption.** Captions any media playing on the phone, with speech models
  that run on the device. It was not part of the 3 September release:
  Google said it was "coming to Pixel devices this fall".
- **Bubbles.** A notification can float as a bubble through `setBubbleMetadata()`.
  In Android 10 it was an experiment behind a developer option.
- **Media and connectivity.** AV1, Opus encoding and HDR10+, audio playback
  capture, a native MIDI API, Dynamic Depth photos, 5G support, the
  `WifiNetworkSpecifier` and `WifiNetworkSuggestion` APIs, Wi-Fi Easy Connect,
  and `DnsResolver` for `SRV` and other records.
- **Dynamic System Updates.** A device can download a Generic System Image
  (GSI), boot it from a new dynamic partition as a guest system, and switch
  back without touching the installed one. Google enabled it on the Pixel 3
  and newer from Beta 4.
- **Smaller additions.** A thermal API in `PowerManager`, Neural Networks API
  1.2, generational garbage collection in ART, `foregroundServiceType`, and
  Vulkan 1.1 as a requirement on 64-bit devices.

## Security and privacy

- **Mainline.** Android 10 is the first release that updates system
  components through Google Play ("Google Play system updates"). The first
  modules include ART, Conscrypt, the DNS resolver, DocumentsUI, Media,
  Networking, PermissionController and time zone data. Some use the new APEX
  format, and a module set installs or rolls back as one unit.
- **Encryption for every new device.** The Android 10 CDD requires devices
  that launch with it to encrypt user data with file-based encryption, on by
  default after setup. Devices without AES instructions may use Adiantum,
  the new encryption mode Android 10 added. Google measured it at about five
  times the speed of AES-256-XTS on an ARM Cortex-A7.
- **TLS 1.3 by default.** Its cipher suites cannot be disabled, and 0-RTT is
  not supported. Certificates signed with SHA-1 are no longer trusted, and
  six SHA-2 CBC cipher suites were removed.
- **Non-resettable identifiers.** IMEI, serial number and similar IDs need
  `READ_PRIVILEGED_PHONE_STATE`, which Play apps cannot hold. What to do is under "What changed for your
  app".
- **MAC address randomization.** Devices send a randomized MAC address to
  Wi-Fi networks by default. Device owner apps can still read the factory
  address with `getWifiMacAddress()`.
- **Background activity starts.** An app in the background can no longer bring
  an activity to the front on its own. It should post a notification instead.
- **Hardening.** System binaries map their code execute-only, so reading it
  crashes with `SIGSEGV`. Media codecs gained bounds and integer-overflow
  sanitizers, and the Scudo allocator and ShadowCallStack arrived. Smart Lock
  can only extend an unlock, never unlock a locked phone, and for at most four
  hours.
- **Smaller changes.** WPA3 and Wi-Fi Enhanced Open, and face authentication
  in `BiometricPrompt`. The new limits on the clipboard and `/proc/net` are
  under "What changed for your app".

## Permissions

Location now has three answers: all the time, only while the app is in use, or
deny. "In use" means an activity is visible or a foreground service of type
`location` is running.

```mermaid title="Location access for an app targeting API 29"
flowchart TD
  start([App reads location]) --> visible{Activity visible, or a location foreground service?}
  visible -- yes --> fg[ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION or ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION is enough]
  visible -- no --> bg{ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION granted?}
  bg -- yes --> ok[Location delivered]
  bg -- no --> none[No location]
```

Android 10 added four permissions an app can declare and changed what several
older ones give:

| Permission                                                                 | What changed in API 29                                                                                                            | What to declare                                                                                    | What to remove or limit                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION`                                               | New runtime permission for location while no activity is visible and no `location` foreground service runs; geofences need it too | Declare it only for a feature that needs background location; request it after foreground location | Nothing; for apps targeting 28 or lower the system adds it |
| `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`                                                     | Needed for Wi-Fi, Wi-Fi Aware, Wi-Fi Direct, Bluetooth and cell scanning APIs; `ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION` is no longer enough       | Declare and request it if you scan                                                                 | Nothing; `WifiNetworkSuggestion` needs no location         |
| `ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION`                                                    | New runtime permission; without it, the location in photos' EXIF data is redacted                                                 | Declare and request it if you read where a photo was taken                                         | Nothing                                                    |
| `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION`                                                     | New runtime permission for the step counter and step detector sensors and Play services activity recognition                      | Declare and request it                                                                             | Nothing                                                    |
| `com.google.android.gms.permission.ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION` (Play services)   | Apps targeting 28 or lower that declare only this one get the platform permission granted automatically                           | Keep it; Google's Transition API guide still declares it                                           | Nothing                                                    |
| `USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT`                                                   | New normal permission, granted at install; without it the system ignores a full-screen intent                                     | Declare it if you call `setFullScreenIntent()`                                                     | Nothing                                                    |
| `READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE`                                                    | Not needed for the app's own files or the media it created                                                                        | Declare it only to read other apps' media                                                          | Nothing yet; Android 13 caps it                            |
| `WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE`                                                   | Under scoped storage it gives no write access outside the app's own files                                                         | Keep it for Android 9 and lower, and for the legacy storage mode                                   | `android:maxSdkVersion="29"`                               |
| `READ_PHONE_STATE`                                                         | No longer gives IMEI, serial number or similar IDs; those need `READ_PRIVILEGED_PHONE_STATE`, which Play apps cannot hold         | Keep it only for other phone state                                                                 | Remove it if device IDs were its only use                  |
| `READ_FRAME_BUFFER`, `CAPTURE_VIDEO_OUTPUT`, `CAPTURE_SECURE_VIDEO_OUTPUT` | Signature only                                                                                                                    | Use `MediaProjection`, which asks the user                                                         | Remove them; regular apps never get them                   |
| `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW`                                                      | Cannot be granted on Android 10 (Go edition); apps that held it before the upgrade keep it                                        | Check `Settings.canDrawOverlays()` before drawing                                                  | Nothing                                                    |
| `CAMERA`                                                                   | Now needed for the device-specific fields of `getCameraCharacteristics()`                                                         | Request it before reading that metadata                                                            | Nothing                                                    |

One manifest for an app that runs on Android 9 and earlier and on 10 and
later. Keep only the blocks your app needs:

```xml
<manifest xmlns:android="http://schemas.android.com/apk/res/android">
    <!-- Location: foreground on every version; background is only
         requested on API 29 and higher -->
    <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION" />
    <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION" />
    <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION" />
    <!-- Storage: write access only where scoped storage does not apply -->
    <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE" />
    <uses-permission
        android:name="android.permission.WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE"
        android:maxSdkVersion="29" />
    <!-- Unredacted photo locations, from API 29 -->
    <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION" />
    <!-- Activity recognition: the Play services and the platform permission -->
    <uses-permission android:name="com.google.android.gms.permission.ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION" />
    <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION" />
    <!-- Calls and alarms that use full-screen notifications -->
    <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT" />

    <application>
        <!-- Location while in use, from a foreground service -->
        <service
            android:name=".NavigationService"
            android:foregroundServiceType="location" />
    </application>
</manifest>
```

Lint `ProtectedPermissions` flags signature permissions such as the three
screen-capture ones in a manifest. Google's media guide uses
`maxSdkVersion="29"` on `WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE`, so the permission still
covers an app in legacy storage mode on Android 10.

Location grants survive the upgrade from Android 9. An app targeting 29 that
declares `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION` keeps foreground and background access;
one that does not keeps foreground only. If you raise the target to 29 while
the app is already installed on Android 10, the background grant is revoked.
The first time an app reads location in the background, the system reminds the
user with a notification.

Apps targeting API 22 or lower show a permissions review screen the first time
they run on Android 10. Apps can no longer look up how permissions are grouped
in the UI.

## Migrating to API 29

From `targetSdkVersion 28` to 29, in this order:

1. **Update the toolchain.** Set `compileSdkVersion 29` and use Android Studio
   3.5 or later. Skipped, `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION` and the other new
   constants do not compile. Verify: the project builds and lint runs clean of
   new errors.
2. **Move file access.** Keep your own files in `getExternalFilesDir()`, write
   shared media through `MediaStore`, and open other files with the Storage
   Access Framework. Stop using `getExternalStorageDirectory()` and
   `getExternalStoragePublicDirectory()`, which Android 10 deprecated.
   `requestLegacyExternalStorage="true"` buys time on Android 10 only.
   Skipped, reads and writes outside your own files fail. Verify: lint
   `ScopedStorage`, then a fresh install on an Android 10 device without the
   legacy flag.
3. **Split location.** Declare `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION` only if a feature
   needs it and request it after foreground location; give location foreground
   services `foregroundServiceType="location"`. Skipped, background location and
   geofences stop. Verify: on Android 10, answer "Allow only while using the
   app" and run every location feature with the app in the background.
4. **Drop hardware IDs.** Replace IMEI, serial and similar IDs with resettable
   ones. Skipped, those calls throw `SecurityException`. Verify: lint
   `HardwareIds`.
5. **Fix Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and cell code.** Request `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` for
   scans, open the Wi-Fi settings panel instead of `setWifiEnabled()`, and add
   networks as suggestions. Skipped, scans and network changes stop working.
   Verify: run each connectivity flow on Android 10.
6. **Declare `USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT`** if you show incoming calls or alarms.
   Skipped, the system ignores the full-screen intent. Verify: no
   `fullScreenIntent` warning in logcat.
7. **Check native code and reflection.** No `execve()` from the home
   directory, no text relocations, `ASharedMemory` instead of `/dev/ashmem`,
   public APIs instead of non-SDK ones. Skipped, these fail at run time.
   Verify: lint `BlockedPrivateApi` and `SoonBlockedPrivateApi`, then exercise
   native features on Android 10.
8. **Test the result** on an Android 10 device or emulator with
   `targetSdkVersion 29`. The compatibility framework that toggles single
   changes arrived in Android 11, so here you test the whole target at once.

Skipping several levels at once: do each version's steps in order, starting
from [Android 9](/notes/android-9-pie/) if you target 27 or lower.

## What lasted

- **Scoped storage** became mandatory. Once an app targets Android 11, the
  system ignores `requestLegacyExternalStorage`.
- **Background location** got stricter in Android 11: users enable it with
  "Allow all the time" on a settings page, not in the first dialog.
- **Bubbles** left the developer options in Android 11 and became the
  platform's way to show conversations.
- **Mainline** grew with each release. Google ended Mainline support for
  Android 11 and lower in the fourth quarter of 2025.
- **Version numbers** stayed. Every release since Android 10 is named by its
  number. The dessert stayed an internal codename, though from Android 13 it
  appears in the SDK constant (`TIRAMISU`).

## Worth knowing

- **Why the desserts went.** On 22 August 2019 Sameer Samat wrote that the
  names "weren't always understood by everyone in the global community". In
  some languages L and R sound the same, so "Lollipop" did not obviously come
  after KitKat, and pies and marshmallows are not desserts everywhere.
- **The logo changed too.** The same post moved the wordmark from green to
  black, because the green "was hard to read, especially for people with
  visual impairments". The robot kept only its head, beside the word.

- **Quince Tart.** In an August 2019 interview, Dave Burke and Dan Sandler
  confirmed that the team calls Android 10 "Quince Tart" internally. Burke
  said the public name would have been "Queen Cake".
- **The `Q` constant** carries a quote in AOSP: "Why? Why, to give you a taste
  of your future, a preview of things to come. Con permiso, Capitan. The hall
  is rented, the orchestra engaged. It's now time to see if you can dance."
- **The Easter egg.** On the Android 10 logo screen, you drag the 1 over the 0
  and turn it to about 315 degrees, where it snaps in place. Do it seven times
  and **Icon Quiz** opens, which AOSP describes as "a nonogram-style icon
  puzzle". Its puzzles are system icons.
